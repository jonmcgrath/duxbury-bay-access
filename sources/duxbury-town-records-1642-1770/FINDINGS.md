# Findings: Duxbury Town Records 1642–1770

**Source:** *Copy of the Old Records of the Town of Duxbury, Mass., from 1642 to 1770*, made 1892 by George Etheridge (copyist), printed Plymouth: Avery & Doten, 1893.
- PDF: `copyofoldrecordsd00duxb.pdf` (downloaded 2026-11-09 from https://archive.org/download/copyofoldrecordsd00duxb/copyofoldrecordsd00duxb.pdf)
- Text layer: `copyofoldrecordsd00duxb_djvu.txt` (Internet Archive OCR of the printed book, with occasional OCR errors)

**Source status:** This is a **secondary source**, an 1892 transcription of the original manuscript books. Etheridge notes that some records were destroyed by fire, and that "a very few of the unimportant Town meetings have been omitted." Cite the original volume and page (given in the margin) when it can be checked at the Town Clerk.

**Citation format:** printed page in the 1893 book, then the original record volume and page as shown in Etheridge's margin, then the line number in the text file.

Searched 2026-11-09 for: landing, highway, to the water, beach, flats, cove, Powder Point, Bluefish, Captain's Nook, and modern site names. These modern names have **no hits**: Mattakeeset, Winsor, Jocelyn, Massasoit, Harden, Island Creek, Sagamore, Saquish, Bay Farm. Most of today's landings were probably created after 1770.

## 1. Bluefish River "old landing place" laid out as a common town landing (1715) — strongest lead

- **Date:** May 20, 1715. Laid out by Selectmen Edward Southworth, John Simons Sen., and John Partridge. Recorded by John Wadsworth, Town Clerk.
- **Cite:** printed pp. 107–108; original Vol. a, Page 99 (**verified** 2026-11-09 against the page image `pages/p107.jpg`; the OCR had "'J9"); text lines ~5540–5592.
- **Key text (verbatim):** "...allowing all the ground on the Southerly side of the said fence to bluefish river channel, for a **common landing place for said town**..." A 40-foot highway was laid out from "a place commonly called the old landing place" up to "the road or high way, that leads from Marshfield to Plymouth."
- **Boundary landmarks:** the mouth of a small creek; Abraham Samson Sen.'s stone wall; two great stones on the bank; a run of water from a great salt pond; south of the houses of Ichabod Samson, Edward Arnold and Abraham Samson; a small whitish rock; a big rock on a knoll near Edmond Chanler's meadow (printed spelling "Chanler's", **verified** against `pages/p108.jpg`; the OCR had "Clninler's"); Capt. John Alden's land; Doctor Delanoe's house.
- **Likely modern site:** 09 Bluefish River Landing (numbered 08 before 2026-11-09). *ASSUMED, not verified.*

## 2. Town vote confirming landing rights at the old landing place (1718)

- **Date:** Town Meeting, March 28, 1718.
- **Cite:** printed p. 237; original Vol. a, Page 300; text lines ~12517–12533.
- **Key text:** The town gave Abraham Samson Sen. and his heirs liberty to fence across "the highway lately laid out from the old landing place... up to the country road," with a gate or bars, "so long as he or they shall give liberty to all persons for landing their hay etc at said landing place." Samson promised the town "the benefit and privilege of the said landing place for the landing and carrying away their hay &c **forever**." Signed by Abraham Sampson.
- **Relevance:** This is a written perpetual grant of landing rights to the town. It ties to finding #1.

## 3. Captain's Nook highway and cart way "down to the Bay" through the Standish farm (1715)

- **Date:** March 26, 1715. Laid out by the Selectmen.
- **Cite:** printed pp. 105–107; original Vol. a, Page 97; text lines ~5443–5524.
- **Key text:** The highway is 30 feet wide, from the Captain's Nook (at the partition fence between the farms of Miles Standish and Thomas Delanoe Jr.) up to the Marshfield–Plymouth road. **Memo:** "a convenient cart way is allowed **down to the Bay** through the farm of said Standish, which is also allowed by said Standish's consent."
- **Likely modern site:** WW-06 Miles Standish Home Site (numbered 19 before 2026-11-09), or possibly the Standish Shore and Hicks Point area. *ASSUMED.*

## 4. Captain's Nook highway re-laid with compass bearings, plus a way "down to the salt bay ... forever" (1766)

- **Dates:** Accepted at Town Meeting March 31, 1766 (vote on printed p. **335**; corrected 2026-11-09 from 334, because the OCR garbled the page header). Layout on printed pp. 336–337, recorded by Selectmen Ezra Arnold and John Wadsworth. Text lines ~18270 and ~18313–18394.
- **Key text:** A 30-foot way, surveyed with bearings and distances in rods (e.g. "North 14 degrees West, 46 rods to a stone set in the ground... North 27 rods to the East end of the Nook Gate"). Landmarks include Capt. Wait Wadsworth, Ebenezer Bartlet, the Brewsters, the heirs of Thomas Prince, Israel Silvester, and Joshua Cushing's cooper shop. Also: "there is **a way through Capt. Wait Wadsworth's land, down to the salt bay**, where it may be the most beneficial and least prejudicial to each other, **forever**."
- **Relevance:** This is the first record found with **surveyed bearings**, so it could be plotted. *Modern site mapping is ASSUMED, the same as #3.*

## 5. Powder Point road and the cart way into Powder Point (1767–1768)

- **Cite:** printed pp. 338–339; text lines ~18468–18497.
- 1767: the town voted that the selectmen "lay out the way that leads from Plymouth into Powder Point."
- March 14, 1768 (original Record No. 5, Page 101): Major Alden was allowed to set a gate across "the high way that leads to Powder Point near the dwelling house of Amaziah Delanoe's, provided the said Alden gives or allows a good cart way... into Powder Point."
- **Likely modern sites:** 06 Powder Point Landing / 07 Powder Point Bridge (numbered 05/06 before 2026-11-09), for road access only. *ASSUMED.*

## Context: Duxbury Beach title (1767)
- Printed p. 338 (corrected from "337–338"). A committee (Briggs Alden, Ezra Arnold, Wait Wadsworth) found that General Winslow and Edward Winslow "have no legal claim or title to said beach." This is not one of our sites, but it shows the town asserting its title to the shore.

## Also found while checking pages: Joseph Drew dam on Bluefish River (1766)
- Printed p. 335. At the Town Meeting of March 31, 1766, "the town gave liberty to Joseph Drew and his partners to build a dam and Gristmill on Blue fish river." This is context for Site 09 Bluefish River (formerly 08) and is linked on that page.

## Out of scope (not Duxbury Bay)
- 1701 highway to the North River at the "Brick Kilns" past "land purchased for a landing place" (printed p. 95, Vol. a p. 35). This is the North River.
- A reserved half-acre "landing place" on the Indian Head River (Stockbridge lot, ~line 5752). This is the Indian Head River, today Pembroke/Hanover.
- "Palmer's Landing place" on the North River (~line 2683).
